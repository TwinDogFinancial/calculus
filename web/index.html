Welcome to Calculus

Calculus is the mathematical study of change. It has two fundamental branches:

  • Differentiation – deals with rates of change and slopes of curves.
  • Integration – deals with accumulation of quantities and areas under curves.

History

Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz (1646–1716) independently discovered calculus in the late 17th century, around the same time as Isaac Newton. Leibniz introduced the integral sign “∫” (derived from the Latin word “summa”) and the differential notation “dx”, which have become the standard symbols used worldwide. His work on the fundamental theorem of calculus linked differentiation and integration, laying the groundwork for modern analysis. The famous priority dispute between Leibniz and Newton sparked intense debate, but today both are credited with the invention of calculus.
